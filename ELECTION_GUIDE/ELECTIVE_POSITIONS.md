# Guide to Elective Positions in Nigeria

A clear, simplified guide to all elected political offices in Nigeria, who elects them, how many seats exist, and how representation works from the federal level down to your local ward.

---

## 🗺️ Quick Overview: The Hierarchy of Representation

In Nigeria, government is structured into three tiers: **Federal**, **State**, and **Local**.

```
🇳🇬 FEDERAL LEVEL
 ├── Executive: President & Vice President
 └── National Assembly (Legislative)
      ├── Senate: 109 Senators (3 per state + 1 for FCT)
      └── House of Representatives: 360 Federal Constituencies

🏢 STATE LEVEL (36 States)
 ├── Executive: Governor & Deputy Governor
 └── Legislature: State House of Assembly (State Constituencies)

🏘️ LOCAL GOVERNMENT LEVEL (774 LGAs)
 ├── Executive: Local Government Chairman & Vice Chairman
 └── Legislative Council: Councillors (Wards)
```

## 🗳️ Direct Elective Positions at a Glance

These are the positions that Nigerian citizens directly vote for on ballot papers:

| Tier | Office / Position | Represents | Who Votes | Total Seats Nationwide | Managing Electoral Body |
|---|---|---|---|:---:|---|
| 🇳🇬 **Federal** | **President** & **Vice President** | The entire Federation | All registered voters nationwide | **1** (Joint ticket) | **INEC** |
| 🏛️ **Federal** | **Senator** | Senatorial District | Voters in the district (3 districts per state + 1 FCT) | **109** | **INEC** |
| 🏛️ **Federal** | **House of Representatives Member** | Federal Constituency | Voters in the federal constituency | **360** | **INEC** |
| 🏢 **State** | **Governor** & **Deputy Governor** | The entire State | All registered voters in the state | **36** (Joint ticket) | **INEC** |
| 🏢 **State** | **State House of Assembly Member** | State Constituency | Voters in the state constituency | **993** | **INEC** |
| 🏘️ **Local** | **LGA Chairman** & **Vice Chairman** | Local Government Area | All registered voters in the LGA | **774** | **SIEC** *(INEC for FCT)* |
| 🏘️ **Local** | **Councillor** | Electoral Ward | Registered voters in the ward | **~8,812** | **SIEC** *(INEC for FCT)* |

## 👤 Who Represents You? (Your Representation Chain)

Every Nigerian citizen living anywhere in the country is represented by **six distinct elected officials**:

1. **President & Vice President** (Federal Executive)
2. **Your Senator** (Federal Legislature — Upper Chamber)
3. **Your House of Representatives Member** (Federal Legislature — Lower Chamber)
4. **Your State Governor & Deputy Governor** (State Executive)
5. **Your State House of Assembly Member** (State Legislature)
6. **Your Local Government Chairman & Ward Councillor** (Local Executive & Council)

## ⚖️ Direct Elections vs. Internal Legislative Leadership

An important distinction in Nigeria's democracy is between **offices elected by the public** and **presiding officers elected internally by legislators**:

### 1. Directly Elected by the Public
Voters go to the polling unit and cast ballots for individual candidates or party symbols representing their constituencies (e.g., electing a Senator or a House of Representatives Member).

### 2. Internally Elected Presiding Officers
The general public does **not** vote for presiding legislative officers. After legislators are sworn in, they elect leaders from among themselves:

| Chamber | Presiding Officers (Elected Internally by Members) |
|---|---|
| **Senate** | **Senate President** & **Deputy Senate President** |
| **House of Representatives** | **Speaker of the House** & **Deputy Speaker** |
| **State House of Assembly** | **Speaker of the Assembly** & **Deputy Speaker** |

> **How it works:**
> 1. Citizens elect a Senator from their district.
> 2. That Senator takes an oath of office in the National Assembly.
> 3. The 109 Senators nominate and vote among themselves to elect the **Senate President**.

*(Other roles like Majority Leader, Minority Leader, Chief Whip, and Deputy Whip are political party caucus leadership appointments, not separate public elections.)*

## 🏛️ Who Conducts Which Elections?

In Nigeria, election administration is split constitutionally between two bodies:

### 1. Independent National Electoral Commission (INEC)
INEC is the federal electoral body responsible for all federal and state-level elections:
- Presidential & Vice Presidential Election
- Senate Elections
- House of Representatives Elections
- Governorship & Deputy Governorship Elections
- State House of Assembly Elections
- **FCT Area Council Elections** (Abuja Chairmen and Councillors)

### 2. State Independent Electoral Commissions (SIECs)
Each of the 36 states has its own SIEC (e.g., LASIEC in Lagos, KANSIEC in Kano) established to organize:
- Local Government Area (LGA) Chairmanship & Vice Chairmanship Elections
- Ward Councillorship Elections

- [x] **10 core elected public positions** (President, VP, Senator, Rep, Governor, Deputy Gov, State Assembly Member, LGA Chairman, Vice Chairman, Councillor).
- [x] **Joint tickets**: President & Vice President run together; Governors & Deputy Governors run together; LGA Chairmen & Vice Chairmen run together.
- [x] **Presiding officers** (Senate President, House Speaker, State Assembly Speakers) are selected internally by lawmakers, not on general election ballots.
- [x] **INEC** runs federal, state, and FCT council elections; **SIECs** run local government elections across the 36 states.
